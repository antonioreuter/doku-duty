Proposal · the story's surfaces

Where the narrative
actually shows up

A fair question. The arc in docs/NARRATIVE-PROPOSAL.md had almost nowhere to appear — so here are the six screens that carry it. Two of the four characters had never been drawn at all.

What the story had, before this page. Ace appeared in the character strip and the reward beat. That is all. The Sheriff — the entire emotional arc, the one who withholds for forty nodes — had no avatar and no screen. Number Four appears nowhere in the codebase at all. The tally wall, the thing that makes progress visible as story, was a line in the gags list. The telegrams (D-62) fire on every load and did not exist. A narrative with no surface is a document, not a game.

Why these three

  • D-61
    The tally wall is the season clock
    Thirteen chalk marks on Ace's wall, sitting on the map where you cannot miss them. It already had to be cosmetic — this makes it mean something without it ever carrying consequence. Biscuit kicks marks off after a clean solve.
  • new
    “Trail progress” becomes “Ledger refiled”
    Identical number, identical bar, identical data. One label turns a completion metric into the case being solved. This is the cheapest narrative change in the entire proposal.
  • new
    A signpost names the act
    Diegetic, not a chapter card: a leaning plank reading The pursuit. It tells a returning player where they are in the story using the scenery that was going on the map anyway.
  • D-18
    No data moves
    Chests at 11, 21, 32, 42 and bosses at 22 and 41 are untouched. The acts sit on the layout that already exists.

02 · The telegram — every load, in character

D-62 has been in the bible since the frontier rewrite and has never been built. Loading is currently dead time; this is the highest-frequency, lowest-cost personality surface in the product, and it is where the case advances between nodes.

Why it carries the story

  • D-62
    One line each, a pool of hundreds
    Already decided, already budgeted, never shipped. Rotating notices — a stolen ledger, mandatory numeracy retraining, a stern word about hitching posts.
  • new
    This is where the plot moves
    A telegram between nodes is the only place the case can advance without interrupting play. Act-gated: the Act I pool is procedural, the Act III pool is close to panic. Same slot, same cost.
  • D-02
    STOP does the heavy lifting
    Telegraphese is naturally clipped, so the six-word rule stops being a constraint and starts being the format. It also translates cleanly — there is no idiom to lose.
  • constraint 5
    It must never gate the first frame
    This is decoration over a load that was happening anyway. If there is nothing to wait for, it does not appear.

Why it reads this way

  • D-22
    “Nothing to lose” is printed on the screen
    Retries are unlimited and free, so say so, in green, above the button. A boss screen that looks like a boss screen will make some players close the app — and the one thing this product sells is that it cannot punish you.
  • §5.1
    A confrontation with no weapon in it
    Ace is pleased to see you. The tension is entirely social, which is the only kind this game is allowed and the funnier option regardless.
  • new
    “Not yet” is a real button
    Backing out of a boss must be as easy as entering it, and must cost nothing. An unskippable confrontation is a failure state wearing a hat.
  • D-71
    Only Ace gets a state colour
    Sunset red is documented as “errors, duplicates, Ace”. Every other character sits on a surface token, which is why he reads as the antagonist instantly and why no ninth hue is needed.

The two arcs closing

    The case goes on the wall and the territory rotates. The rank is permanent and the Sheriff is keyed to it, so his approval survives the season that earned it.
  • D-59
    Biscuit gets an action, never a bubble
    His beat is drawn with a dashed frame and stage direction, not speech — the wordless rule made visible in the layout so nobody is ever tempted to give him a line.
  • gags

    Whatever Ace collected across six weeks, Biscuit clears it at the close. The warmest beat in the game and it costs one animation.
  • new
    Ace's exit line is season two's opening
    “I'll want that back” makes rotation feel like an episode ending rather than content being taken away.

The rule that keeps him legal

  • D-60
    Unhelpful in tone, never in content
    Four objects to working conditions and placement aesthetics — never to correctness. He is never wrong, because no character in this game is ever allowed to be. The caption under the strip states it so a player never second-guesses a correct move.
  • D-42
    He makes the duplicate gag land
    Digits reacting to each other in horror is the replacement for the conventional red flash. It only works if the numbers have already been established as people, and Four is the character that does that establishing.
  • note
    No face at avatar size
    I tried giving the numeral eyes and a mouth at 46px and it read as a pair of goggles. A bold outlined digit is unmistakably Four and survives the size; the face belongs in the full rig, where there is room for it. Worth remembering for the whole cast — avatar crops are silhouettes, not portraits.
  • D-40
    He lives below the grid like everyone else
    A digit that talks while you solve risks the spirit of the never-cover-the-grid rule even from the strip. Open question in the proposal: does Four ever speak from inside a cell? My answer is no.
