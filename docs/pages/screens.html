screen specifications

Nine screens define the product. Everything else is a variation on one of them. Annotations cite decision IDs argued in docs/DECISIONS.md; exact values are canonical in docs/DESIGN.md.

  • D-04
    No mistake limit
    Non-punitiveAuto-check off by default. Errors counted quietly for stats, not flagged. Strict mode is opt-in.
  • §8
    Five-column pad, not nine
    Nine keys in a row cannot carry the depth treatment and are worse for thumbs regardless. Bigger targets, fewer per row. The markup above drew nine and the build is five — five is right, and it is why erase stays a digit key rather than a tool (D-100).
  • D-100
    Tools are named, never drawn
    CorrectedThis row was specified from the start and built as six bare symbols — ✎ ~ ⁙ ↶ ✕ ✓ — because it never became a requirement. ✕ beside ✓ read as cancel beside confirm, which advertised the answer-checking D-04 refuses. Now named in words.
  • D-101
    Absent, not disabled
    The default pad is three tools. The ink control appears with notes mode; the two that end a chain test appear only while there is a test to end. A control that looks live and swallows the tap is the failure state constraint 1 objects to — an empty space is not.
  • §4
    Pressed state is visible
    Notes is active: dropped 4px, depth reduced to 1px. Eight pixels of travel is enough to feel and not enough to lag.
  • D-40
    The strip is a stage, not a container
    Empty most of the time. Character slides up, holds 2.5s, leaves. Reactions never queue and never cover the grid.
  • D-24
    No interstitial reachable from here
    Ad ruleNothing interrupts a grid in progress. Ever.

Notes and candidates

The system the top three ranks are built on. Nine candidates per cell in fixed positions, two inks, and highlighting that turns a wall of small digits into a readable pattern.

  • D-09
    Nine candidates, not three
    A three-note cap makes Marshal upward literally unplayable. Naked triples and X-wings need full candidate sets.
  • D-10
    Fixed positions
    1 top-left, 5 centre, 9 bottom-right, always. Players scan by position rather than reading. Notes that reflow are the hidden cause of an app feeling bad.
  • D-11
    Two inks
    DifferentiatorTurquoise marks a hypothesis under test, cleared in one tap when a chain fails. Almost nobody ships this.
  • D-13
    Candidate highlighting
    Tap a digit, every cell holding it lights up. Pointing pairs become visible instead of deduced.
  • D-52
    Notes are silent
    No particle, no sound, no haptic. Forty entries a minute would turn any celebration into noise.

  • §7
    Ruby gives monetisation a face
    A store and a pass offer with no character reads as software. Same screen either way, and one version is warmer. She owns the store, the pass, and chest openings.
  • D-27
    Pass inclusion drives annual
    Nine seasons at $4.99 is $44.91. The annual plan undercuts it and folds in everything else.

    Unlimited hints are safe to give
    Solo game, no opponent, no fairness to protect. Withholding them buys nothing but resentment.

    Offered at node 5
    After the player has progress worth protecting. On first open it reads as a paywall on a free game.

Screen 07 · personnel

The cast

Five characters, each holding a role the others do not. Expression lives in eyes and brows only — everything else is fixed, which is what makes the rig affordable across nine territories a year.

  • D-37
    Roles, not slots
    Antagonist, counterweight, ally, wildcard, commerce. A character without a job is one nobody writes lines for.
  • D-38
    Biscuit never speaks
    Zero localisationA horse reads as western at 30px where a burro does not. Same wordless role — identical comic timing in every market.
  • §7
    The bandana is a budget decision
    Rig economyCovering Ace's mouth means no mouth shapes, halving his rig cost and making lip-sync a non-question in fifteen languages. Design element and cost decision are the same decision.
  • §7
    Ruby owns monetisation
    The store, the pass offer, and chest openings. A paywall becomes a conversation with someone who has seen every deputy come and go.
  • D-39
    The Sheriff is always wrong, never harmful
    Following his advice wastes a move, never a puzzle. A wrong hint with real cost turns a gag into a trap.
  • D-35
    Five is over scope, stated plainly
    v1 was scoped at one character, then four. This is five, and it is real additional art. The art budget is the product budget.

Screen 08 · reward feedback

Completion effects

Particles come from the world, not a generic sparkle library. Rows are lassoed, blocks are detonated, and mistakes are noticed rather than punished.

  • §6
    Lasso for rows, dynamite for blocks
    A rope cinches along the row — an unmistakably western verb that reads as collected. Blocks detonate radially so they never read as a row.
  • §6
    No firearms
    ExcludedPunishment as an aesthetic undoes fifty-five decisions. It also pushes the age rating to 9+ or 12+, restricts ad networks, and forfeits the family audience. Dynamite passes because it celebrates rather than threatens.
  • D-42
    Digits react instead of flashing red
    SignatureSame information as the red flash, delivered as character. Fires constantly, costs one animation, converts the sorest moment into the most charming one.
  • D-47
    Cascades chain at 90ms
    Lasso, lasso, dynamite — brighter and a tone higher each step, Biscuit stamping on the third. 520ms total, a deliberate exception to the 400ms rule.
  • D-48
    Tints never stack
    A cell in three completed units looks like a cell in one. Additive tinting darkens exactly as the board most needs to stay readable.
  • §6
    Particle budget
    8 puffs per row, 12 sparks per block, 40 scraps at completion. 60 on screen maximum. Cascades reuse the pool rather than tripling it.

Screen 09 · colour

Palette and state roles

The palette is eight tokens: six carry state — ink, focus, candidate, provisional, error, completion — and two are surfaces (D-53 as amended by D-71). Every hue has one job, and decoration draws from that set rather than adding a ninth. Values are canonical in docs/DESIGN.md.

  • D-53
    One hue, one job
    RuleSix states need reserved colour, and two surfaces complete the eight (D-71). Decoration borrows from this set, never adds to it.
  • D-54
    Two hues the brief did not ask for
    Brown, yellow, orange and green cannot carry five states — too close in hue to separate on a dense grid. Turquoise and night indigo are authentically southwestern. The dusk-purple outline stops the warm hues collapsing into each other.
  • D-55
    Territories repaint surfaces, never states
    A new territory may change bone, sand, and props. The six state colours are fixed across every season, so the grid is never relearned.
  • §4
    Depth shades are derived, not new
    Every offset shadow is a darker value of its own hue. No shadow introduces a colour that is not already in the set.
  • D-50
    Colour is never the only signal
    Corner marks on completion, position on notes, character animation on errors. The board stays legible with the hues stripped out.
